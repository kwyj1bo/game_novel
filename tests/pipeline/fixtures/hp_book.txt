CHAPTER ONE
The Letters

The kitchen at number four, Privet Drive, smelled of burnt toast. Uncle Vernon sat at the table behind his newspaper while Aunt Petunia fussed over Dudley.

A letter slid through the front door and landed on the mat.

"Get the post, boy," said Uncle Vernon, without looking up.

Harry walked to the front door and picked up the letters. One envelope was thick and heavy, and it was addressed to him in green ink.

"There's one for me," Harry said.

Mr. Dursley lowered his newspaper. His face turned purple. "Give that here!" he shouted.

Dudley laughed and pointed at Harry.

Harry grabbed the letter and held it behind his back. Uncle Vernon lunged across the kitchen and snatched it out of his hand.

"No one is writing to you," Mr. Dursley hissed. "Go to your cupboard."

Harry stood in the kitchen doorway, shaking with anger.

CHAPTER TWO
The Alley

Hagrid led Harry through the crowded alley. Shop windows glittered on both sides of the cobbled street, full of cauldrons, owls and broomsticks.

"Welcome to Diagon Alley," said Hagrid, grinning down at him.

Harry stared at everything. A witch was selling dragon livers from a stall, and a crowd of boys pressed their noses against a shop window.

"First, the bank," Hagrid said. "Can't buy anything without gold."

At the end of the street stood a white marble building. Harry followed Hagrid up the steps and into Gringotts.

The marble hall was enormous. Goblins sat at long counters, weighing coins and writing in ledgers.

"Mr. Potter wishes to make a withdrawal," said Hagrid. He pulled a tiny golden key from his coat.

A goblin peered down at them. "And does Mr. Potter have his key?"

Harry took the key from Hagrid and held it up.

The goblin nodded slowly. "Very well. Follow me."

CHAPTER THREE
The Forest

The Forbidden Forest was black and silent. Tall trees closed over the path, and the moon barely reached the ground.

"Stay close," Hagrid whispered. "There's something out here that's been hurting the unicorns."

Malfoy stopped walking. "I'm not going in there," he said. "My father will hear about this."

Hagrid's huge hound growled and pressed against his legs.

They reached a clearing. Something silver lay on the ground, very still.

A hooded figure crawled out of the trees and bent over the silver shape.

Malfoy screamed and ran back down the path. The hound bolted after him.

Harry did not run. He raised his wand and stepped towards the clearing.

The hooded figure lifted its head and glided towards Harry. A searing pain burst through his scar.

Harry fell to his knees, clutching his forehead.

Hoofbeats thundered through the trees, and the hooded figure fled into the dark.
